# Copilot Support — Legal Team: What Happened and What To Do Next

*Issued by DWP IT Service Desk | 2026-08-12*

This update explains each reported Copilot issue in plain English and lists the next steps for the affected user.

---

## Ticket 1 — Paralegal: "I don't have access to that content" for a client NDA in SharePoint

**What this means**

Copilot can only use files you are allowed to access right now. If the NDA is in a folder you have never used before, you may not actually have permission to that file or folder yet. In some cases, the file may also have a security label that allows normal viewing but blocks Copilot from summarising it.

**What to do next**

1. Open the file in SharePoint and confirm you can open it directly.
2. If you cannot open it, request access from the folder owner or matter owner.
3. If you can open it but Copilot still says no access, send the file link and timestamp to IT Service Desk so we can check label/policy settings.
4. Retry after access is granted or after IT confirms policy status.

---

## Ticket 2 — New associate: Copilot in Outlook cannot find case emails

**What this means**

For new starters, Copilot in Outlook can take time to catch up with mailbox content. Your account and email data usually need 24 to 72 hours to be indexed before Copilot can use them well.

**What to do next**

1. Continue using Outlook normally and try Copilot again over the next 1 to 3 days.
2. If there is no improvement after 3 working days, contact IT Service Desk and reference this ticket.
3. Include 1 to 2 example email subjects Copilot should have found.

---

## Ticket 3 — Partner: Copilot summarised a draft settlement from another matter

**What this means**

Copilot is likely showing content because your account currently has permission to that folder or file (possibly through inherited or group access). This is a permissions issue, not usually a Copilot malfunction.

**What to do next**

1. Do not share or forward the surfaced content.
2. Report the file path, matter name, and time seen to IT Service Desk immediately.
3. IT and Legal Security will review and remove any unintended access.
4. You may be contacted for a short follow-up while access logs are reviewed.

---

## Ticket 4 — Legal ops manager: Entire Legal team lost Copilot access this morning

**What this means**

A team-wide loss of access is usually caused by a licensing or policy change, or less commonly by a Microsoft service issue. This is being handled as an active service incident.

**What to do next**

1. No individual user action is required right now.
2. Continue work using standard Microsoft 365 apps while restoration is in progress.
3. Watch for service updates from IT on status and estimated restore time.
4. If a critical deadline is blocked, raise a priority ticket and reference the Legal Copilot incident.

---

## Ticket 5 — Contract specialist: Copilot gives generic answers on contract template clauses

**What this means**

Generic answers usually mean Copilot is not grounding on your template library content. This can happen if access is limited, indexing is still catching up, or prompts are too broad.

**What to do next**

1. Confirm you can open the relevant template documents directly in SharePoint.
2. Ask a more specific prompt, for example: "Using Template X, summarise termination clause 8 and list fallback language."
3. If templates were updated recently, wait up to 24 hours and retry.
4. If results remain generic, send IT two sample prompts and the template links you expected Copilot to use.

---

## General Advice for Legal Users

1. Copilot follows your existing Microsoft 365 permissions. If access is too broad or too narrow, Copilot output will reflect that.
2. For sensitive matters, always verify source document permissions and labels.
3. When reporting issues, include: app used, exact prompt, time, and expected document location.
