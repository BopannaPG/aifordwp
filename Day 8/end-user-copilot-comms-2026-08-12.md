# Copilot Support — What's Happening & What To Do Next

These responses have been prepared for each person who raised a Copilot support request today. Please read the section that matches your ticket.

## Ticket 1 — Finance Lead: Copilot won't summarise the Q3 board pack

**What's happening**

Even though you can open the file yourself, Copilot works differently to you. The Q3 board pack most likely has a security label (such as "Confidential" or "Highly Confidential") applied to it. These labels are there to protect sensitive documents, and one of the things they can do is prevent Copilot from reading or summarising the content — even for people who are allowed to open the file normally.

It is also possible the file was uploaded or updated very recently and Copilot simply hasn't had time to process it yet (this can take up to 24 hours).

**What to do next**

1. Check the sensitivity label shown at the top of the document when you open it in SharePoint or Word.
2. If it shows "Confidential" or "Highly Confidential", contact the IT Security team to confirm whether Copilot processing is permitted for that label tier.
3. If the file was uploaded or changed in the last 24 hours, try again tomorrow.
4. If neither of the above applies, reply to your support ticket and we will investigate further.

## Ticket 2 — New Hire: Copilot doesn't seem to know about my recent emails

**What's happening**

This is completely normal for a brand-new account. When a new Microsoft 365 account is created, it takes between 24 and 72 hours for the system to finish indexing your emails and other content so that Copilot can reference them. Until that process is complete, Copilot has very little to work with, so its answers will seem limited or generic.

**What to do next**

1. Give it a little time — try Copilot again tomorrow or the day after.
2. If it has been more than three working days and Copilot still can't reference your emails, contact the IT Service Desk and we will check that your Copilot licence has been fully set up.
3. No action is needed from you right now — this is expected behaviour and should resolve on its own.

## Ticket 3 — HR Manager: Got "I don't have access to that content" in Word

**What's happening**

The error message you received is Copilot's way of telling you that the file is protected and it is not allowed to read it. Salary review documents are very likely marked with a high-level security label specifically to stop tools — including Copilot — from processing sensitive HR data. This is a deliberate protection, not a fault.

**What to do next**

1. Open the spreadsheet and check the sensitivity label at the top of the file.
2. If the label says something like "Highly Confidential – HR", that label is intentionally blocking Copilot access.
3. If you need to analyse that data using Copilot, speak to your HR data owner and the IT Security team about whether a lower-protection working copy can be created, or whether an alternative approach is available.
4. Please do not attempt to remove or change the sensitivity label yourself.

## Ticket 4 — Sales Rep: Copilot in Teams can't find the client contract

**What's happening**

The contract was shared with you via a guest link from another organisation's system. Copilot can only search and reference files that live inside DWP's own Microsoft 365 environment. Content that is stored in an external organisation's system and shared with you via a link sits outside the boundary that Copilot is allowed to access — this is by design and applies to everyone.

**What to do next**

1. Ask the external contact to email you a copy of the contract, or to upload it to a shared Teams channel or SharePoint site within the DWP environment.
2. Once the file is saved inside DWP's own storage, Copilot will be able to find and reference it (allow up to 24 hours for it to be indexed).
3. This is not a fault with your account — Copilot simply cannot reach outside our organisation's systems.

## Ticket 6 — Manager: Copilot showed me a file I didn't know I had access to

**What's happening**

Copilot is working exactly as it should here. It can surface any file or document that you have permission to access, even if you have never opened it or have forgotten it exists. Your permissions may include inherited access to certain folders — for example, because you are a member of a team or security group.

This is not a fault, but it is a useful prompt to review whether your folder access is still appropriate.

**What to do next**

1. You do not need to raise a fault — Copilot has not done anything wrong.
2. If you are concerned about having access to a folder you no longer need, contact the owner of that SharePoint site or your line manager and ask them to remove your access.
3. If you are unsure who owns the folder, contact the IT Service Desk and we can help you find out.

## Ticket 7 — Analyst: Copilot only gives generic answers, doesn't use internal content

**What's happening**

When Copilot gives generic answers rather than drawing on internal documents, it usually means it cannot find any internal content linked to your account. The most common reasons are that your account has limited access to SharePoint sites, or that your access to those sites was set up recently and the content hasn't been indexed yet.

**What to do next**

1. Open SharePoint in your browser and check whether you can browse and open documents on the internal sites you would expect to use. If you cannot see them, you may need to request access.
2. If you can see the SharePoint content but Copilot still isn't using it, try again after 24 hours — indexing can sometimes take time.
3. If the problem continues after 48 hours, contact the IT Service Desk. Make a note of which SharePoint sites you expect Copilot to draw on — this will help us investigate more quickly.

## Ticket 8 — Executive Assistant: Copilot in Outlook can't see the shared mailbox calendar

**What's happening**

Copilot in Outlook is currently only able to work with your own personal mailbox and calendar. It does not have the ability to access shared mailboxes or calendars that you manage on someone else's behalf — even if you have full delegate access. This is a known limitation of how Copilot works at the moment, not a fault with your account or setup.

**What to do next**

1. For now, you will need to manage the shared mailbox calendar directly in Outlook rather than through Copilot.
2. Microsoft is aware of this limitation and support for delegated mailboxes is expected in a future product update.
3. If this is causing significant disruption to your work, please raise it with your line manager so it can be logged as a business requirement for future Copilot planning.
4. If you believe your situation is different from what is described above, contact the IT Service Desk with further details.
